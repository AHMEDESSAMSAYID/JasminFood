Food is a regional trade, not a national one

"Turkish" tells you almost nothing. Apricots come from Malatya, figs from Aydın, hazelnuts from the Black Sea coast, apples from Isparta and Karaman, eggs from Afyonkarahisar. Each has its own season, its own grading language and its own way of being cheated. No office anywhere sources all of that by email — you have to be in the right region in the right month, and that is what this desk exists to do.

How we work

Six steps, from your enquiry to the port

Each step has one owner and one clear output that reaches you.

1

We take in the enquiry

The item, the grade, the quantity and the destination. In food the season is part of the enquiry: what is available in October is not what is available in May.

2

We shortlist three suppliers

We rule out anyone your market's requirements do not fit before showing them to you — no time wasted on a supplier whose goods will not clear.

3

We visit and photograph

A real visit to the farm or the packing line in its season, not a phone call and not catalogue photographs. You see what you are buying before you pay.

4

We send the offer, sample and analysis

A written offer carrying the full specification — grade, moisture, packing — with a sample and a laboratory analysis where the item calls for one.

5

We inspect and measure before shipment

Counting, measuring and weighing on the actual goods, firmness or moisture readings by item, and a photographed report before the doors close.

6

We follow through to the port

Health certificates, batch analyses, the booking and the container temperature record, followed daily to the port of arrival.

Why us

For every familiar problem, a defined step

These are not general promises. Every line is something we actually do on every shipment.

The goods arrive a grade below the sample you approved.
We count, weigh and measure a sample from the actual consignment rather than from the offer sample, and photograph it before the doors close.
You buy "Malatya apricots" or "Turkish hazelnuts" and have no idea what grade.
We do not accept an offer without a written specification — grade, moisture, packing and batch number — so you compare figures, not the names of regions.
The goods arrive sound and then stop for a missing document.
We review the destination's requirements and the establishment registration before booking, not once the container is on the quay.
The reefer arrives at a temperature nobody agreed to.
We require a data logger inside the container and ask for its record at discharge, so any dispute rests on a document rather than on recollection.
The supplier stops replying, or the negotiation stalls on language.
We negotiate in Turkish and follow up daily, and go to the site ourselves when it is needed.
You cannot tell what the middleman is actually taking off the source price.
We work on a commission agreed in advance. Nothing hidden is added to the price you see.
