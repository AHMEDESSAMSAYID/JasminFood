DRIED FRUIT

Dried apricots and figs

This is the category Türkiye is known for worldwide. Malatya produces the overwhelming majority of the world's dried apricots, and Aydın is the home of the Sarılop fig on which the entire international fig trade rests. Reputation is not the same as specification. In apricots the difference between one grade and the next is measured in pieces per kilo, flesh colour and moisture; in figs it is measured in size, pressing method and an aflatoxin result. Anyone selling you "Malatya apricots" with no grade number and no analysis attached is not selling you a specification. They are selling you the name of a region.

APRICOT

Dried apricots

There are two types in the trade and no third: sulphured, treated with sulphur dioxide, holding a bright stable orange; and natural, untreated, dark brown, more expensive and sold into a different market. The choice is not a matter of taste. The permitted sulphur dioxide level differs from market to market, is stated in an analysis attached to each shipment, and must match the destination's limit before loading rather than after. Grading is by pieces per kilo — the lower the number, the larger the fruit and the higher the price — starting at Jumbo and working down. Moisture normally sits in the low twenties; above what was agreed you are buying water by weight and inviting spoilage in store.

FIG

Dried figs

The Sarılop variety from Aydın — Germencik, İncirliova, Nazilli — is the base. The recognised trade classifications distinguish fruit pressed in even layers, fruit packed loose, and fruit pulled by hand, each with its own size, price and market. The real file on figs is not size, it is aflatoxin: it is behind most rejections of Turkish fig consignments worldwide. Serious packers in Aydın run the fruit under ultraviolet sorting, because contaminated fruit fluoresces under it and is then pulled by hand. We ask for that sorting step to be documented, and for an aflatoxin analysis from an accredited laboratory on each batch rather than a general certificate for the plant.

PACKING

Packing and supply formats

Wholesale packing is normally 5 and 10 kg cartons with a food-grade inner liner; retail runs 200–500 g packs printed with the importer's brand. The liner is not a detail: dried fruit breathes and takes up odours, and a carton without a proper liner arrives smelling of the container. For long voyages or hot destinations we ask for vacuum or modified-atmosphere packing, and we fix in the purchase order that the stated weight is net and not gross — a recurring dispute that surfaces on the first scale in the importer's warehouse.

LAB

The analyses we ask for

In this category we do not work from a general certificate framed on the factory wall. For each batch we ask for an accredited laboratory analysis covering aflatoxin, sulphur dioxide, moisture and pesticide residues, carrying the same batch number printed on the carton. We also ask for a health certificate and a certificate of origin, and for anyone importing into Saudi Arabia the Saudi Food and Drug Authority requirements for the item are reviewed before booking. Labelling follows GSO 9 and shelf-life periods follow GSO 150 — and remaining shelf life is counted from arrival, not from loading.

What we need from you, and what we verify on your behalf

What we need from you to start pricing

The clearer the enquiry, the sharper the shortlist.

  • The item: apricots or figs, and which grade or classification
  • Sulphured or natural, and the SO₂ limit you accept
  • Whole, pitted or diced
  • Pack weight and format
  • Quantity in tonnes or containers
  • Whether you want your own brand printed
  • Destination country, so we can check the limits applied there

What we verify before loading

Inspection on the actual goods, with a photographed report reaching you before the container leaves.

  • The grade verified by counting pieces per kilo
  • Flesh colour and its consistency across cartons
  • Moisture measured on a sample at packing
  • A valid analysis carrying the same batch number
  • Documented UV sorting for figs
  • Inner liner intact and properly sealed
  • Net pack weight on a scale, not as printed
  • Goods free of insects and any sign of them
